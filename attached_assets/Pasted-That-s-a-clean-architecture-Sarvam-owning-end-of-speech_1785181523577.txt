That's a clean architecture — Sarvam owning end-of-speech authority while local VAD stays out of the audio-gating path is exactly the right separation, and the fact that you already hit and fixed the actual failure mode (gating during playback starving Sarvam, calls 772→1320) is good evidence it's not just theoretical.

A few things worth flagging:

The silently-broken "Endpointing silence" setting is the real issue here. A UI control that does nothing on Sarvam agents but doesn't tell anyone is worse than no control at all — someone (you, Gopi, or a future team member) will tune that slider during a bad call, see no effect, and either waste time debugging the wrong layer or conclude the whole endpointing system is flaky. Two options: gray it out / hide it for Sarvam-backed agents, or make it actually route into Sarvam's vad_signals params. Given you already have high_vad_sensitivity and the fast-flush wired up, exposing the real knobs (silence floor, hold-on-mid-thought threshold) under that same UI slot for Sarvam agents seems more useful than a no-op holdover from Azure.

The ~0.6s turn-taking floor is worth knowing exactly where it comes from, since you've broken it into three stacked pieces (0.5s Sarvam silence floor, up to 1.2s mid-thought hold, up to 300ms start-speaking delay). The 1.2s hold is your biggest lever if you ever need to shave latency — that's the Tamil half-sentence protection, so it's presumably tuned deliberately, but worth knowing if a customer ever complains about lag whether that's the first thing to revisit or whether it's already as tight as it can go without breaking the language-quality trade-off.

request_id logging closing the loop is the right call — that's the one item that had no workaround, so good that it's now wired into all three surfaces (transcript logs, error logs, dashboard exec log) rather than just one.

One thing I'd sanity check rather than take on faith: confirm the "never flushes Sarvam" claim under a real barge-in test, not just code review — i.e. actually interrupt an agent mid-sentence a few times and watch whether the local VAD's barge-in detection stays purely on the TTS-cutoff side and never touches the Sarvam socket. That's the kind of invariant that's easy to accidentally violate in a later edit (someone adds "just one line" for an edge case) without a comment or a test guarding it.
