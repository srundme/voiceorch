# Pre-Seed Pitch Deck

At pre-seed there is usually **no product revenue**. The deck sells **vision, problem, team, and possibility**. Investors bet on people who can turn an idea into meaning.

## How pre-seed differs

- Emphasize vision, mission, problem, and team charisma  
- Inspire first; hard metrics later  
- Keep it short — no P&L theater  
- Tailor packaging by region: e.g. Japan often prefers denser bullet detail; many Western rooms prefer visual storytelling  

## Craft rules

1. **Tell a story** — emotion, drive, ambition; first impression is memory, not spreadsheets.  
2. **Keep it short** — assume ~3 minutes of skim time.  
3. **Aesthetics & fonts matter** — legible standard fonts (e.g. Helvetica); large headings (~100pt class for titles when appropriate).  
4. **Practice the delivery** — mirror, video, speak each slide without notes; practice seated if Zoom.  

Color note: blue often reads as trust/calm on covers.

92% of successful decks worldwide include a **“why now?”** section — do not skip it.

Competition slide gets heavy attention pre-seed (~55s vs ~34s at seed in cited research) — show you have thought it through.

## Recommended 13 slides

### 1) Purpose / Cover
Who you are, why you’re here, aesthetic tone for the whole deck.

### 2) Problem
Problem statement early — beating heart of the story.

### 3) Solution
What you do to solve it; must connect clearly to the problem (avoid product-for-product’s-sake).

### 4) Why now?
Timing: market inflection, category sales crossing a threshold, IPOs, consumer outcry, tech enablers.

### 5) Market size
TAM/SAM with proof. Niche is fine if you can dominate it — need proof, not only a huge top-line TAM.

### 6) Competition
Visual SWOT / landscape; strengths, weaknesses, **your differentiation**.

### 7) Product
No live product? Use design flows / mockups that make the core interaction obvious (Tinder swipe example).

### 8) Market validation
LOIs, expert quotes, waitlist, first design partner, community interest, press — tactical validation without scaled usage.

### 9) Business model
Revenue model + pricing; if innovative pricing, explain why it is superior and how you protect against abuse.

### 10) Go-to-market
Distribution hypotheses + first marketing bets (e.g. influencer design partners) even before product ships.

### 11) Financials
Educated, realistic estimate from market × problem × pricing — not fantasy hockey sticks.

### 12) Roadmap
3–5 year realism: launch, next raise, key marketing — do not promise an app in weeks.

### 13) Team
Often move **forward** in the deck. Pre-seed is a people bet: vision, charisma, “this team could make anything work.”

Vary order by audience; US often rewards narrative arc; some markets want denser financials.

## Reference decks to study (patterns, not templates to copy)

- **Castle** — clear problem → solution → numbers → roadmap; raised ~$270k pre-seed  
- **Bluewire** — 9 slides; sharp “audience is shifting / we sit in the center” story  
- **Manpacks** — visual “flip book” problem/solution; community + press as proof; ~$500k  

## Living document

- Seek feedback every pitch; build variants for scenarios  
- Research prior investments of the target; adapt structure without losing authenticity  
- Speak to humans: long-term 5–7 year outcome, FOMO on a great team, not a quick flip  

## Final pre-seed reminder

Investors want the next exceptional founder signal. Make the deck impossible to ignore — then refine relentlessly.
