# Seed Fundraising (YC-style practical guide)

Distilled for founders raising the **first institutional / serious seed**. Not a full legal treatise — basics only.

## Why raise

High-growth startups usually need to burn capital before profit. Cash is a competitive advantage (hiring, PR, marketing, sales). Fundraising is often brutal — but necessary for most.

## When to raise

Raise when you can tell a compelling story: market opportunity, customer, product fit, and growth that impresses.

- Some raise on reputation + story alone  
- Most need idea + product + traction  
- Impressive growth example: **~10% week-over-week for several weeks** (context-dependent)  
- Rule: **when you can raise, you usually should**

Until then: talk to users and build.

## How much to raise

Ideal: enough to reach **profitability** (or next fundable milestone in 12–18 months for hardware / deep tech).

Tradeoffs: progress purchased, investor credibility, dilution.

- Target **~10–20%** dilution; avoid **>25%**  
- Tie the ask to a believable plan; show success at full raise *and* at a smaller raise (different growth speed)  

**Engineer rule of thumb (all-in):** ~$15k / engineer / month  
Example: 5 engineers × 18 months ≈ **$1.35M**  
Common seed asks often cluster ~**$500k–$1.5M** (wide variance; rounds have been trending larger).

Answer “how much?” with: **N months of runway** (usually 12–18) and a range for $X under different plans.

## Instruments

Silicon Valley seed is often **SAFE** or convertible note; priced equity is rarer early (more expensive/slower).

### Convertible note

Debt that converts in a priced round. Terms: principal, interest (~2%+), maturity, usually **cap** and/or **discount**. Cap/discount = seed premium. Can be called at maturity (extensions common).

### SAFE (YC)

Like convertible debt **without** interest, maturity, or repayment. Negotiable: amount, cap, discount. Prefer standard YC SAFE docs. Read the SAFE primer for conversion examples.

### Equity (priced)

Set pre-money valuation → price/share → issue shares. Example:

- Raise $1M on $5M pre, 10M shares → $0.50/share → sell 2M shares  
- Post = $6M; dilution = 2M/12M = **16.7%** (not 20%)

Know option pools, liquidation preferences, anti-dilution, protective provisions when you price a round. Hire a lawyer.

**Always prefer well-known, founder-fair standard docs.**

## Valuation

No formula for two hackers + early users. Market sets price via investor demand. Caps on SAFEs/notes are notional valuations.

- Don’t over-optimize; get a deal you can live with that clears the raise with acceptable dilution  
- Seed valuations often roughly **$2M–$10M** range historically — ranges move; closing matters more than maxing the number  

## Angels vs VCs

- **Angels:** amateurs (own money); faster; emotional; $25k–$100k+ common tickets  
- **VCs:** pros (LP money); more meetings; partners; few investments from many seen  
- Warm intros beat cold email; Demo Day concentrates attention  
- Also: micro-VCs / super-angels, AngelList syndicates, FundersClub-style vehicles  

Crowdfunding (AngelList, Kickstarter, Wefunder, etc.) often fills or reanimates rounds; rarely the whole plan unless exceptional.

## Meeting investors

Goal of first meeting: **get the next meeting**, not close on day one.

- Research what they invest in and why  
- Simplify: great product (demo), right team, huge company dream  
- Listen more than you talk — connection matters for a multi-year relationship  
- Practice relentlessly; style that fits you  
- Confidence + humility; never arrogance; no defensiveness  
- Always leave with an attempted close or crystal-clear next steps  

Optimize for **money soonest** (breadth-first, prioritize likely closers). First check is hardest; momentum follows.

## Negotiating & closing

Seed can close fast on SAFEs. Negotiate few variables (cap, maybe discount). Take complex asks offline; get counsel.

Once they say yes: **handshake protocol** — docs + wire immediately (Clerky/Ironclad-style). Delay after yes is usually the founder’s fault.

## Documents for seed

Don’t overbuild diligence. Red flag if they demand heavy financials too early.

1. **One-page exec summary:** vision, product, team, contact, traction, market, raise to date + current ask  
2. **Slide deck** leave-behind: visuals > text walls  

Suggested deck parts: company/tagline → vision → problem → customer → solution/why now → market → landscape → traction → model → team → summary → fundraising/roadmap (≤6 quarters).

## Rules to follow

- Raise, then return to product — but don’t stop too early if hard  
- Be greedy on process: many parallel conversations, prioritize EV  
- High ethics; Valley is small  
- Learn investor “no”; leave doors open  
- Stay organized; thick skin  

## What NOT to do with investors

Dishonesty · arrogance · over-aggression · indecision (OK to say “don’t know”) · filibustering · slow follow-up · broken agreements · fake financials · silly TAM · fake expertise · NDAs early · playing investors off each other without skill · real-time negotiation · obsessing dilution/valuation · taking no personally · spending the meeting on minutiae  

## Glossary (short)

- **Cap:** max effective valuation for note/SAFE holder  
- **Discount:** % off priced round for early holders  
- **Pre-money / post-money:** value before / after new cash  
- **Dilution:** ownership % decrease from new shares  
- **Pro-rata:** right to maintain ownership % later  
- **Liquidation preference:** preferred gets money out before common on exit  
- **TAM:** total available market  
- **Lead:** first/largest who brings others in
