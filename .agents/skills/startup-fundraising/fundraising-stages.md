# Fundraising Stages, Investors & Timelines

## Why raise

90% of startups fail; running out of cash is a top-3 reason. Fundraising is not only capital — it is the right partners, timing, and story.

External funding accelerates growth, hiring, marketing, product, and runway when bootstrapping cannot reach scale (especially capital-intensive markets).

### Capital cycle

1. Capital infusion (equity for cash)
2. Growth investment
3. Valuation increase
4. Further fundraising on better terms
5. Exit (acquisition / IPO)

Most successful startups raise multiple rounds, each unlocking the next milestone.

## Stages (typical ranges)

| Stage | Typical raise | Usually when | Sources |
|-------|---------------|--------------|---------|
| Pre-seed | ~$31k–$625k | Idea / limited traction | Friends & family, angels, accelerators |
| Seed | ~$625k–$2.5M | MVP + early traction | Angels, accelerators, early VCs |
| Series A | ~$2.5M–$18.75M | PMF + growth metrics | VCs |
| Series B | ~$18.75M–$62.5M | Significant traction | VCs / growth |
| Series C+ | ~$62.5M–$125M+ | Substantial revenue, path to profit | Late VC, PE, hedge, corporate |

- **Pre-seed:** bet on team + vision; validate assumptions; build MVP.
- **Seed:** refine product, expand team, show PMF signals.
- **Series A:** scale ops; need business model, unit economics, traction — not just a cool product.
- **Series B:** accelerate in proven markets; new markets / product lines.
- **Series C+:** market dominance or exit prep.

## Investor types

### Angels

- Personal capital; often ~$12.5k–$625k
- Pre-seed / seed; faster decisions (weeks)
- Mentorship + connections
- Pros: speed, flexibility, risk appetite
- Cons: limited follow-on, uneven professionalism, smaller network

### VCs

- LP capital; ~$319k–$127M+ by stage
- Thesis-driven; multi-partner process; board involvement; seek 10x+
- Pros: size, follow-on, network, signal
- Cons: slow diligence, control rights, growth pressure, timeline mismatch

### Crowdfunding

- Often ~$66k–$6.6M; consumer-facing; storytelling + marketing
- Pros: community, validation, marketing lift
- Cons: audience needed, admin load, fees, possible signaling risk for later VC

Rule of thumb: match stage to investor type (angels/accelerators early; institutional VC for A+).

## Fundraising timeline (plan for 3–6 months)

| Phase | Duration |
|-------|----------|
| Preparation (deck, model, data room, targets) | 4–8 weeks |
| Discovery & outreach | 2–4 weeks |
| Pitch & initial meetings | 3–6 weeks |
| Due diligence | 2–6 weeks |
| Term sheet & negotiation | 1–3 weeks |
| Closing | 2–4 weeks |

Runway rules:

- 12–18 months of runway from each round
- Start next raise with 6+ months runway left
- Full process often 3–6 months

### Milestones by jump

- Pre-seed to Seed: MVP, team roles, early adopters, initial unit economics hypotheses
- Seed to A: PMF metrics, repeatable acquisition, clearer unit economics, core team beyond founders
- A to B: strong YoY growth (often 2–3x), optimized acquisition, market position, expansion plan
- B to C: often $10M+ ARR (SaaS), efficient scale, path to profit, infrastructure, leadership in core segments

### Calendar

- Stronger: Jan–Jun, Sep–Nov
- Weaker: Jul–Aug, Dec–early Jan
- Watch conference bottlenecks and VC fund deployment cycles

## Dilution (typical)

| Round | Dilution |
|-------|----------|
| Seed | 15–25% |
| Series A | 15–30% |
| Series B | 15–25% |
| Series C | 10–20% |

By Series C, founders often retain 15–30% depending on history and terms. Seed: aim under 20% dilution if possible; avoid over 25%.

---

## Success rates and expectations (directional)

- Many meetings to few serious pitches; often 20–30 pitches per term sheet
- Roughly 5–10% of startups seeking funding close a round
- Female founders historically ~2.3% of VC; diversity gaps persist
- Return targets: angels ~10x / 5–7y; seed VCs 10–15x; A/B 5–10x; growth 3–5x

## Market context notes (use carefully; may age)

Treat macro stats as background, not gospel — verify before citing in a live deck:

- Global VC activity can stabilize after boom/bust cycles; early-stage often more resilient
- Hot themes in recent years: AI, climate, health/biotech, enterprise software
- Investor shifts: more structure, deeper diligence, path-to-profitability, sector specialization

## Fundraising mindset

1. Start with relationships, not asks
2. Focus on business fundamentals
3. Embrace the marathon
4. Know your numbers cold
5. Tell a compelling story
6. Be honest about risks
7. Choose partners, not just checks
